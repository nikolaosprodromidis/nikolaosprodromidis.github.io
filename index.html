About Me

Welcome to my personal website. I am a Browaldh Postdoctoral Fellow at the Unit of Economic History, Umeå University. My research focuses on health economics, labor economics, and economic history.

I study the long-run effects of historical public health and labor market reforms, primarily using Swedish administrative data and quasi-experimental methods. Recent work examines working-time regulations, the expansion of hospital births, and place-based restrictions on youth vaping.

Research

Working Papers

Working Hours and Workers’ Health: Evidence from a National Experiment in Sweden
(with Martin Karlsson and Daniel Kühnle)
Abstract: We study the causal effect of shorter working hours on mortality by exploiting a nationwide Swedish reform in 1920 that reduced the workweek from 55/56 to 48 hours for selected occupations. Using full-population register data and difference-in-differences designs, we find that the reform reduced mortality by approximately 15% in the following years, mainly through fewer workplace accidents and heart-related deaths. Effects are particularly strong for older workers.

School Vaping Bans and Youth E-Cigarette Use
(with Dhaval M. Dave, Jooyoung Kim and Joseph J. Sabia), NBER Working Paper 34805
Abstract: We provide the first evidence on the effect of K-12 school vaping restrictions on youth e-cigarette use. Using multiple nationally representative surveys and a generalized difference-in-differences design, we find little evidence that indoor school bans reduce youth vaping. In contrast, broader place-based restrictions (covering schools, workplaces, restaurants and bars) are associated with meaningful reductions in teen nicotine vaping.
